# Say what to photograph

Two halves. The first is a screen; the second is a ruling.

## 1. The guidance

A first-run sheet on the capture screen, dismissible, recallable from
the header, in priority order — because a crate gets walked at speed
and the ordering is the whole message.

**Fill the frame with the disc label.** Not the disc, not the sleeve
holding the disc: the label. It carries the catalogue number, the
label name and usually the coupling, and it is the one surface that
describes the record in your hand rather than the record the sleeve
was printed for.

**Then the sleeve back**, for the tracklist. **Then the runout**, held
at an angle to the light, where a pressing is identified when nothing
else will do it.

**Then anything ambiguous**, and this is the one worth spelling out:
the sleeve is where the decoys live — a mono and a stereo number
printed as a pair, an LP number beside a tape number, an export number
beside the domestic one, an advert carrying a different record's
number entirely. If the disc and the sleeve disagree, photograph both
and let the matcher find out which is real.

The 2026-08-31 crate — items 467-483 — is the whole argument, and it is
already in the README. This puts it where it is needed, which is
in front of the person holding the camera.

## 2. The downscale, ruled

Item **481**'s catalogue number is printed on the Ace of Clubs badge,
in the right place, and is not in the stored file: it was resized away
at 1568 px. `PHOTO_LONG_EDGE`'s own comment claims "nothing downstream
loses anything", and that is now known to be false.

**Framing is the dominant fix, not pixels.** A whole 12" disc at
1568 px puts the label across ~520 px and a catalogue number at maybe
16 px a character, which JPEG then finishes off. The same label filling
the frame gives ~50 px a character and is never in doubt. Guidance buys
more than resolution does, and costs nothing.

**So: raise the long edge to 2048 px, and no further.** It is ~1.3 MB a
frame against 800 KB, ~70% more in a queue that has to survive a loft
with no signal — and it buys back the margin for a shot framed in a
hurry. Keeping a full-resolution original costs roughly five times the
storage to buy less than the guidance does for free, so it is refused.

**And show the shot back.** Tapping a thumbnail opens it full-size, so
"is that number legible?" is answerable while the disc is still in your
hand — which is the only moment it is cheap to answer.

Neither half helps the 483 rows already shot. That is the mop-up crate,
and CATALOGUE-CONTROLS is where it gets listed.

**Done when** the sheet appears once per device, the long edge is
2048 px, and a photograph can be inspected before the disc goes back.
